Chasing a fancy title broke me.

For a long time, I believed my career was the most important part of my life. I chased titles like Software Architect or Staff Engineer at my last full-time job. But I was at the wrong place, at the wrong time. 

After a couple of years, the honeymoon was over. Work felt like a burden, and I thought a new role would spice things up.

When I discussed the chances of a promotion with my boss's boss, I was told _"You could build any role you want."_ But all my ideas were rejected with a _"Someone is already doing that."_ That subtle rejection frustrated me even more. Months later, [I was laid off]({% post_url 2024-12-05-HowALayoffFeels %}).

I let my work frustrations spread to the rest of my life. I stopped exercising, writing, and eating regularly. I burned out so badly I couldn't get out of bed.

It took about two years to wake up with purpose. [Yes, change is possible]({% post_url 2026-08-23-Affirmation %}).

But burnout and a layoff made me rethink everything. My job and a title aren't the most important parts of my life. I've placed strong boundaries around work and leisure. I code part-time, and only code during working hours. At lunch, I shut down everything work-related and return the next day. Zero emails or notifications. I joke by saying I'm not a passionate coder anymore.

I've learned to diversify my sources of joy and accomplishment. I make sure to [do something I love every day]({% post_url 2026-07-26-Create %}), like walking, reading, and writing. I refuse to make my job my whole life. [That saved me and keeps me from going back]({% post_url 2026-09-01-BurnoutFree %}).
