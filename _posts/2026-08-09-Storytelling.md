Here's what I learned:

**#1. A story is about change over time.** It's about a transformation or realization. _I believed this, then something happened, and now I believe this_.

Unlike an anecdote, a story is something you will remember for days, months, or even years.

> _"A story are the best meals of your life."_

**#2. Make them care.** Nobody wants to listen what you have to say unless you give them a reason to listen.

**#3. Don't describe.** Descriptions consume readers' bandwidth. Instead, leverage people's imagination by choosing words they can already see. People care about what you felt, what you said, and what you did.
	
**#4. When public speaking, don't memorize.** Remember a story by thinking in terms of scenes.

In a good story, scenes are connected to each other. You can't remove anything from the story.

> _"The best performer know what they're going to say, but not exactly how"_

**#5. To make your stories interesting,** use: stakes, suspense, surprise, and humor.

**#6. Different versions of stakes in a story:**
* Elephant: What get people's attention initially.
* Backpack: Hopes and dreams you're loaded with along the way.
* Breadcrumbs: Clues or hints along the way.
* Hourglasses: Once you have your audience, make them wait.
* Crystal balls: Out-loud predictions.
* Humor: To make your audience feel at ease, make them laugh in the first 30-60 seconds of your keynote.

**#7. Don't waste your openings by overexplaining.** Use location, action, or wonder. Start in the middle of action. Remember BABC.

**#8. To have an arc,** make your beginnings opposite to your endings.

**#9. To make a good trailer or teaser,** make sure people understand the problem the character is facing but don't solve the problem or let us know if the problem is going to be solved.

**#10. How to improve at storytelling:**
* Strategically listen. Don't just be a consumer. Instead notice why something works.
* Understand what a story is. Once you do that, you'll realize they're everywhere.
* Ask what you can say to keep the person listening. Get through the boring parts by making it suspenseful or funny.
