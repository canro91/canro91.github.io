_"Aren't you writing that much anymore?"_ he asked me.

I was catching up with a friend over some fruit juice and lemonade.\
That question made me frown and ask why he said that.

_"I meant on LinkedIn. I've noticed you started to write about coding. And I thought, 'Ah, he just got boring'."_

It wasn't the first time someone brought up my LinkedIn posts in a random conversation, without seeing their likes or comments—[Most readers don't engage or react]({% post_url 2026-10-17-Write %}).

For months, I used LinkedIn to share anything I found interesting.\
Career advice, writing lessons from TV shows, coding tips, books I was reading...\
I was "testing" what worked on the platform and what resonated the most with my readers.

But in recent months, to copy the strategy of a best-selling author, I've started to separate subjects and interest per platform:\
LinkedIn for coding.\
Substack for wellness and personal growth.
But [my blog is still my catch-all place]({% post_url 2025-05-02-Blogging %}).

I thought readers—I don't want to say followers—were interested in only one of the subjects I wrote about.\
And restricting subjects per platform was an easy way to "build a consistent brand," especially on LinkedIn, around [my coding books](/books).\
But I was wrong.

_"I only use LinkedIn. That's where I find people and follow writers, professors... And they share their random thoughts too."_

Now I see the meaning of the popular line _"You're the niche."_\
It's our interests and weird side what truly attract readers into our Internet corners.\
Not everyone reads—or follows you—for one single subject.

Don't get boring. Don't hide your weird side.\
That's what some people want to see from you.

_I don't write books about a single subject. On [my books page](/books), you'll find short and actionable books on coding and personal growth._
