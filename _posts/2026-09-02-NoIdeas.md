To write for over 20 years, only think about tomorrow's post.

Seth Godin, the godfather of daily blogging, said on [an interview](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=C9pmYsY1ldo):

> _"I don't post a blog post because I feel like it. I don't post a blog post because it's perfect. I post a blog post because it's tomorrow."_

If you follow your interests, you won't run out of ideas.

Of course, you won't be interested about the same subject for over 20 or 30 years. Years ago, I was deeply interested in coding. These days, I'm more interested in [building a burnout-free live]({% post_url 2026-09-01-BurnoutFree %}). My blog reflects that. Your blog and writing will grow and evolve as you do. [Mine has]({% post_url 2026-07-19-Changes %}).

To write for yeeears, as a rule of thumb:

**#1. If you only have a few sentences, write them.** If they help anyone, share them.

**#2. If somebody else already wrote about it, write it your way.** Your stories, perspective, and voice will make it unique. [Your fingerprint makes anything unique]({% post_url 2024-11-21-PutYourFingerprint %}).

**#3. If you aren't absolutely certain about it, write it anyway.** Once you're certain, write about it again.

That's how I've been doing it every day since November 2024. And when I feel like quitting, I remember [Seth sharing about his 10,000 daily post]({% post_url 2026-07-20-Quitting %}).

_Writing saved me from burnout. That's why I keep doing it. It's one of the [10 Surprisingly Simple Ideas That Changed My Life]({% post_url 2026-03-04-10SimpleIdeas %})._
