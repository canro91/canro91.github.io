I'm obsessed with opening lines.

I dissect and collect them like the crazy cat lady from The Simpsons collects cats.

Here are 3 opening lines I love from recent reads:

## #1. _The Hobbit_ by Tolkien

> _"In a hole in the ground there lived a hobbit."_

Simple but powerful.\
It makes you wonder: Why a hole? And what's a hobbit?

## #2. _Chronicles of a Death Foretold_ by García Márquez

> _"On the day they were going to kill him, Santiago Nasar got up at five-thirty in the morning to wait for the boat the bishop was coming on."_

[I've dissected one of García Márquez's opening lines]({% post_url 2025-09-25-OpeningLine %}), but this one is my favorite.\
It makes you wonder: Who is going to kill him? Why? Why did he wake up at 5:30? What does the bishop have to do with it?\
I'm sold after that line.

## #3. _The Subtle Art of Not Giving a Fuck_ by Mark Manson

> _"Charles Bukowski was an alcoholic, a womanizer, a chronic gambler, a lout, a cheapskate, a deadbeat, and on his worst days, a poet."_

I didn't expected to find such a good opening line in a self-help book.\
It uses surprise and contrast:\
After listing everything that makes Bukowski sound like a loser, "a poet" is the last item, as if it were his worst quality.\
Genius!

_I hope studying opening lines will help me write better ones myself. You can find [my books](/books) and judge for yourself._
