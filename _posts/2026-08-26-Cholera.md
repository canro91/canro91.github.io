I just finished reading Gabriel García Márquez's _Love in Times of Cholera._

Here are five drops of wisdom I couldn't help but underline (my own translations):

**#1.**

> _"Wisdom comes when it's no longer of any use."_

I've often heard people say, "I wish I knew what I know now five years ago."

**#2.**

> _"The weak would never enter the kingdom of love, it is a harsh and miserly kingdom, and women surrender only to men of brave spirit, because they inspire the security they so desperately crave in life."_

A wise friend has less stylistic version, _"coward men don't date attractive women."_
Replace the nouns appropriately.

**#3.**

> _"Say yes. Even if you're dying of fear, even if you later regret it, because you'll regret it all your life if you say no."_

OK, dating advice an aunt shared with her niece in the story.
_"Live without regrets,"_ [a friend told me]({% post_url 2026-07-30-NoRegrets %}), the other day.

**#4.**

> _"The world is divided between those who shit well and those who shit badly."_

I've witnessed that division.
Here are [10 habits I follow to improve my digestion]({% post_url 2026-02-22-Digestion %}).

**#5.**

> _"The problem with marriage is that it ends every night after making love, and it has to be rebuilt every morning before breakfast."_
