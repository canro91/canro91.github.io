Eight years ago, I published my first post—without any idea what I was doing

I was starting my coding career, and one day I googled "how to get better at coding."
I kept finding "start a blog."
So I did.

I built a blog on top of my GitHub account.
I still host my blog there, [after all these years]({% post_url 2026-07-18-EightYearsOfBlogging %}).

## The early days

My first post was a tutorial to compile hours of googling.

OK, "tutorial" is a strong word. It was like a word vomit.
Here's [my very first post]({% post_url 2018-07-18-AOPWithFody %}), still unchanged as a reminder.

My blog was a public notebook.
[I wrote about what I was learning at work]({% post_url 2024-12-06-AlwaysWriting %}).
It was a 100% coding blog.

I didn't have any schedule or intention.
I only did to [improve my coding skills]({% post_url 2025-10-28-StreetSmartCoding %}).

## The rise and fall

Thanks to _Income School_ on YouTube, I learned about SEO optimization.

I learned to play the SEO, aiming at Google's first page.
I changed my style: one post every other week about a cluster of topics.

To drive readers to my blog, I shared my posts on dev.to and on LinkedIn.
I still reshare on dev.to and [I recently ran an AMA to celebrate my anniversary]({% post_url 2026-07-25-AMA %}).

[My blog replaced my portfolio]({% post_url 2025-01-12-BlogVsPortfolio %}) and earned me my first paycheck online.

I kept showing up until 2023, when burnout made me quit for months.

## The comeback 

In 2024, already burned out, I was laid off. [I felt lost]({% post_url 2025-01-07-DiversifyYourJoy %}).

With lots of free time, I jumped back to writing as therapy.

I took [my first writing class]({% post_url 2024-12-26-WriteDaily %}).
I started to write daily. Last June, I hit [my 600-daily-post milestone]({% post_url 2026-06-24-600DailyPosts %}).
I also [adopted a workflow]({% post_url 2024-12-15-BloggingWorkflow %}) and changed [my blogging rules]({% post_url 2026-03-03-BloggingRules %}).
My blog became a time capsule, instead of a coding-only blog.

Writing (and my blog) saved me.
That's [why I keep doing it]({% post_url 2026-04-19-WhyIWrite %}).

My blog was (and still is) the playground and inspiration for [my books](/books).
It's now part of my healthy routine.

But, it hasn't been unicorns and roses.
I've wanted to quit more times than I can count.
Then I remember my heroes.
Among them, [Seth Godin, who's blogged for over 20 years]({% post_url 2026-07-20-Quitting %}).
And I go back to it.

No matter what, I always return to writing and my blog.
It's my longest project and my proudest.

And I can finally call myself a writer, thanks to my blog.
