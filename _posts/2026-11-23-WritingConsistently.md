What if you commit to your craft to make it work no matter what?

That's what Elizabeth Gilbert did as a teenager.\
She's an American journalist and writer, author of _Big Magic_ and _Eat, Pray, Love_.

This time, I watched [her interview with David Perell](https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=r6Gmgr-ytf0), here's what I learned:

**#1. Don't quit your day job... and don't get into debt.**\
I've repeated plenty of times I wanted to quit coding and write full time.

But instead of quitting, what you need is discipline to sit down for 45 minutes and write.\
Just 45 minutes. Nothing less. Nothing more.

When your clock marks 45 minutes, stop, and come the next day.\
And stopping means resting, not scrolling.

**#2. Don't judge a working session by word count.**\
Words written is a bad metric of productivity.\
Instead, track how long you sat down to write and if you do it consistently.

**#3. It's always a hard time to be an artist.**\
It was hard to be a writer in the 90s... and in the 2000s... and in the 2010s... and it's hard to be an artist today.\
Nobody gets into writing (or art in general) because it's fun, easy, profitable.

**#4. _"I don't write novels to write the best novel. I write novels to write the best novel I can write."_**\
Before AI, there were other writers way better than you.\
Don't compare yourself to others, but to yourself.

**#5. If your cup is empty, stop.**\
Just like farming, you can't harvest all year long.\
The earth needs rest, so do you.
